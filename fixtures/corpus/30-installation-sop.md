# Standard Operating Procedure — Whole-House Installation

Northgate Water Systems · Effective 1 March 2026 · Supersedes the January 2025 revision

---

## 1. Before the Truck Leaves

The installing technician confirms, from the job record:

- A raw water test less than 90 days old, including hardness, iron, manganese,
  pH and — where the scope includes radon — a waterborne radon figure in pCi/L.
- The equipment list on the signed estimate matches what is loaded.
- Any permit required by the municipality has been issued. Six of our towns
  require a plumbing permit for a whole-house installation; the office confirms
  this at scheduling, and the technician verifies it before travelling.
- Confirmed access to the main shut-off, and a drain within 20 feet of the
  install location.

An installation started without a current water test is the leading cause of
callbacks in this company. If the test is missing, the job is rescheduled.

---

## 2. On Arrival

1. Walk the install location with the customer before unloading anything.
   Confirm the position of the equipment and the drain route, and get verbal
   agreement.
2. Photograph the existing plumbing at the intended tie-in point. This
   photograph resolves nearly every subsequent dispute about pre-existing
   condition.
3. Test the water at the tap yourself. Field results occasionally differ from
   the lab report, and finding that out now is far cheaper than finding out
   afterwards.
4. Locate and operate the main shut-off. A seized main is a discovery to make
   before the system is cut into, not after.

---

## 3. Installation

- Install a bypass valve on every unit without exception. A system that cannot
  be bypassed turns a routine service call into an emergency.
- Provide an air gap at the drain. A drain line submerged in a standpipe is a
  cross-connection and will fail inspection.
- Support the drain line for its full run. An unsupported line sags, holds
  water, and freezes in an unheated basement.
- Fit sample ports before and after the treatment train. Without them, proving
  the system is working requires dismantling it.
- Label each unit with the install date, the model, and the office telephone
  number.

---

## 4. Commissioning

1. Pressurise slowly and inspect every joint.
2. Run a full manual regeneration on softening equipment and observe every
   stage. Do not leave a site having only started the cycle.
3. Draw a post-treatment sample and test it. Record the result on the job.
4. On radon systems, verify the vent terminates above the roofline and the
   blower runs continuously.

---

## 5. Customer Handover

Walk the customer through, in this order: how to bypass the system, how to add
salt and how much, what noises are normal, and when the first service is due.

Explicitly set expectations for two things, because both generate calls that are
not faults:

- Water may run discoloured for a few hours after installation, as disturbed
  sediment clears.
- On radon aeration systems, **cold water will be noticeably warmer than it was
  before** — see the NG-RN manual. Saying this at handover converts a complaint
  into an expectation.

Leave the manual, the water test, and the completed commissioning record.

---

## 6. Before Leaving

Photograph the finished installation. Confirm the drain is clear and no leaks
are present after 15 minutes at pressure. Close out the job in the field before
driving away — job notes written that evening lose the detail that makes them
useful.
