# NG-RN Radon Water Aeration System — Service Manual

Northgate Water Systems · Revision B

---

## 1. Principle of Operation

The NG-RN removes waterborne radon by spray aeration. Water is sprayed into a
vented tank, radon partitions into the air, and a blower vents it above the
roofline. Treated water collects in the tank sump and is delivered to the house
by an integrated repressurisation pump.

Typical reduction is 96 to 99 percent at flow rates up to 12 gallons per minute.

---

## 2. Warm Water After Installation

**This is the single most common post-installation call, and it is expected
behaviour rather than a fault.**

The system holds roughly 40 gallons in an unpressurised tank at ambient basement
temperature. Before installation the cold supply came directly from the well at
around 50 °F. After installation, water sits in the aeration tank long enough to
approach basement air temperature, typically 62 to 68 °F.

Customers describe this as "the cold water is warm now" or "my water has been
warm ever since the radon system was installed". They are not mistaken and
nothing is broken.

What to ask:

1. Is it the cold tap specifically, and is it consistent rather than
   intermittent?
2. Has anything changed about hot water availability? If yes, the cause is
   elsewhere — the aeration tank does not affect the water heater.
3. Where is the tank installed? A tank in a utility room next to a boiler will
   run warmer than one in an unheated cellar.

What to explain: the temperature change is a consequence of the process, not a
defect, and the alternative — no aeration — means the radon stays in the water.

Where the customer finds it genuinely unacceptable, the options are relocating
the tank to a cooler space, insulating the tank and the run to the house, or a
point-of-use chiller at the kitchen tap. Insulation typically recovers 2 to 3 °F
and is the cheapest of the three.

Do not raise a warranty claim for this symptom.

---

## 3. Routine Service

**Every 12 months.**

- Inspect and clean the spray nozzle assembly. Iron and manganese build a film
  that narrows the spray cone and reduces aeration efficiency long before it
  blocks the nozzle outright.
- Check the blower intake filter. A blocked intake is the most common cause of
  reduced radon removal.
- Verify the vent line is clear and terminates above the roofline with an
  unobstructed cap. A vent that discharges into an attic re-circulates radon
  into the house.
- Test the tank level float and the pump cut-in and cut-out pressures.
- Sanitise the tank annually. The tank is vented and non-pressurised, so it is
  open to airborne bacteria in a way a pressure tank is not.

---

## 4. Faults

### Reduced radon removal

Test the post-treatment water before touching the equipment. If removal has
genuinely fallen, the order of investigation is: blower intake filter, nozzle
fouling, vent obstruction, then blower motor.

### Pump short-cycles

Waterlogged pressure tank on the delivery side. Check the tank pre-charge with
the system depressurised: it should be 2 psi below the pump cut-in pressure.

### Air in the household lines

Normal for the first day after commissioning, as trapped air clears. Persisting
beyond that suggests the tank level is running low enough to draw air, usually a
float adjustment.

### Musty odour

Sanitise the tank. If the odour returns within weeks, check the vent cap — an
open vent without a screen admits insects and organic debris.
